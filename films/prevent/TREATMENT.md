# PREVENT: Before the Change

Style: Sci-fi Hologram HUD (`styles/hologram-hud`), hue red on near-black, accent pale ice.
Length 2:12, 1920×1080, 24 fps, English voice (text-to-speech stand-in until the owner records their own) and burned-in captions.

Source: "PREVENT – The Film: Project Document" (16 Nov 2026). Its story and facts are kept. Two of its style rules are overridden by the owner's later request for a 3D film in red and black: "no 3D" and "dark blue-grey, red only for a change going wrong".

## 1. Three candidate structures

- **A. The document's eleven scenes, cut to seven.** Dots, then a graph, then a loop. Clear, but it is a slide order: the viewer never sees one thing change.
- **B. One part, twice.** A single invented part stands on the projector pad for the whole film. The first time, a late change to one dimension breaks everything downstream. The second time, the same moment arrives and PREVENT speaks first. Everything between the two is the explanation of why the second time is different.
- **C. Rewind.** Open on the damage and scan backwards to the moment before it. Strong hook, but a rewind needs explaining and the document allows no jargon or cleverness that needs explaining.

**Chosen: B**, carrying the document's dots → graph → loop image inside it. The same part and the same dimension are seen failing at 0:04 and being corrected at 1:05, so the one-sentence message ("a warning that arrives before the change is made") is shown, not said. The opening is the fault itself, the ending is a power-down to the word mark, and the score is built around two silences: one before the warning, one when the loop breaks.

## 2. Logline and arc

A small late change to one part breaks everything after it; the knowledge to stop it was already in the company, and once connected it speaks up before the change is made.

- **Setup**: a part is scanned in. One dimension faults. The fault runs down the line to the drawing, the parts list and the station. It is not new, and the knowledge is scattered in three piles.
- **Turn**: the piles connect into one graph. The same part returns, the same dimension is locked before it faults, and nothing downstream is touched.
- **Ending**: the graph serves three outputs, lives only as a loop with the engineers, and starts with a bounded pilot. Power-down to the word mark.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Instrument-panel briefing films* in science-fiction cinema (the tactical readout that explains a plan in a minute): **learn** how one object stays on screen while attention jumps by target box, and how a value "locking" replaces a cut. **Don't take** any craft, emblem, voice or screen layout.
- *Kurzgesagt-length explainer pacing*: **learn** one idea per sentence, picture arriving half a beat before the word. **Don't take** its characters, colours or music.

## 5. Beat sheet

- 0:00 part scans in from the pad. 0:03 the hole's dimension goes hot and flickers. 0:05 "ONE SMALL CHANGE."
- 0:07 the fault runs down a floor line; drawing, parts list and station each flicker and break in turn.
- 0:18 everything scan-erases to single dots; earlier dots light up behind along a line. "WE HAVE SEEN THIS BEFORE."
- 0:27 dots settle into three piles: ECRS, RULES, ENGINEERING DOCUMENTS. "THE ANSWER ALREADY EXISTS."
- 0:36 edges begin to draw between the piles. 0:40 the title rolls in like a value: PREVENT.
- 0:44 the graph lifts off the floor and turns, still growing. "ONE KNOWLEDGE GRAPH."
- 0:55 the part scans back in under the graph. 1:00 silence. 1:02 target lock on the hole, leader, card: "SEEN BEFORE. CHECK THIS HOLE."
- 1:06 the value rolls and locks in the accent colour. The line downstream stays calm. "BEFORE THE CHANGE IS RECORDED."
- 1:20 three beams leave the graph: design tool warning, KPI dashboard, learnings back to the EPT stage. Chips dock.
- 1:38 a ring of plain figures stands round the graph; light flows both ways. 1:46 one link breaks, the stage dims, silence. 1:49 it reconnects.
- 1:55 1,000 / 1,000 / 70–80 % roll and lock. 2:00 power-down; the word mark and the closing line hold to 2:05.

## 6. Cue map

96 bpm, 4/4, one bar = 2.5 s. Key D minor.

| Section | Bars | Music | Lands on a downbeat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fault | 1–7 | projector hum, sparse square pulse, low bass on the bar | fault at bar 2; each downstream break |
| Scattered | 8–14 | half-time; pad only, single plucked arp notes | piles labelled |
| Graph | 15–22 | arp enters with ping-pong delay, hats, 8th bass | title lock at bar 17 |
| Warning | 23–32 | arp thins; bar 25 true silence; lock = drop and wide saw chord | target lock; value lock at bar 27 |
| Outputs | 33–39 | full: arp, bass, hats, claps; a layer per beam | each beam |
| Loop | 40–46 | steady; bar 43 silence when the link breaks | reconnect |
| First step | 47–50 | longer note values, decelerating; one sustained tone | each value lock; power-down |

Captions change on the voice, not the grid.

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fault | projector hum 40/80/120 Hz, room tone | scan sweep, fault buzz, three breaks (falling blips) | sparse, uneasy |
| Scattered | hum | scan-erase sweep, dots landing as soft ticks | half-time pad |
| Graph | hum | edges drawing as rising blips in key, title roll ticks, lock clack | arp builds |
| Warning | hum, then nothing | lock thump + clack + two-tone beep, leader blip, roll ticks, value lock | silence, then drop |
| Outputs | hum | three beam whooshes, chip docks | full |
| Loop | hum, then nothing | flow ticks, break (falling tone), reconnect beep | steady, silence, back |
| First step | hum fading | roll ticks, three locks, power-down sweep | sustained tone |

Sound as transition: the scan-erase sweep starts before the camera cranes (J-cut into "Scattered"); the last chord of "Outputs" rings over the ring of figures arriving (L-cut).

## 8. Type and captions

Rajdhani, all caps, for labels, cards and the title; Share Tech Mono for micro data. Every text block on its own dark plate. Captions are the style's HUD plate with corner brackets and voiceprint bars, bottom centre, near-white. The title is part of the scene and rolls in like a value. English only, so each line stands alone in the upper line's place.

Words on screen follow the document: no real ECR numbers, part numbers, product or person names; pilot figures shown as plan figures. "NX" and "EPT" are named in the outputs shot as the document assumes; both live in `content.json` and can be made generic in one edit.
